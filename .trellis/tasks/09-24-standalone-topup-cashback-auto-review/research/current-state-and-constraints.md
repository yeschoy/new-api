## Stage-1 feasibility gate — read-only source inspection

**Conclusion:** ordered durable grants + one authoritative final wallet balance suffice for *unreversed, nonnegative, settled FIFO spending*, including late cashback grants, but do **not** establish the requested chronological attribution for all normal billing histories. No refund product code should be written on the assumption that a request-lifecycle marker alone closes the gap. This is a source-level finding plus deterministic arithmetic, not a three-database runtime verification.

### Conditional proof and arithmetic

Let `G=[g1,...,gn]` be all committed, actually available quota segments in wallet-credit order, including an opening nonrefundable balance, and let `Q` be an authoritative nonnegative final balance. If every other committed wallet mutation is a plain FIFO spend, there is no unsettled reservation, special withdrawal, or unexplained adjustment, then `S=sum(G)-Q` units have been spent. The remaining units are exactly the suffix of `G` with total size `Q`: spending removes a prefix and appending a grant adds a suffix. Induction over those two operations shows that intermediate ordinary spend timestamps need not be stored. A same-transaction purchase and instant gift must get stable purchase-before-gift order; a delayed gift is appended only when `issueLockedCashbackRewardTx` actually credits the wallet (`model/cashback_state.go`), **not** at order/payment time. Segment order must come from one committed serialized sequence, not Unix seconds or the order of inserts made before acquiring the user wallet lock.

Arithmetic checked with a local, file-free Python calculation: A, `[old purchase 100, gift 10, new purchase 50, gift 5]`, final `Q=40` leaves `new purchase 35, gift 5`; B, same grants and `Q=140`, leaves old purchase 75 + new purchase 50 and gifts 15; delayed gift `[old purchase 100, new purchase 50, old gift 10]`, `Q=55`, leaves new purchase 45 + gift 10. Other gateway grants and admin/redeem grants must also occupy nonrefundable segments at their *actual* credit position; a missing grant can wrongly increase the reported remaining Epay purchase. An opening mixed balance may only be a nonrefundable starting segment, never an automatically refundable historical purchase. Example verified CNY 90 paid / 100 purchased / 50 unused yields 45 paid units; use integer cents and cumulative target differences for partial refunds, never independently round every retry.

### Counterexample: later reversal of an earlier reservation

The same ordered grants and final balance can have different historical purchase attribution. History H1: grant old purchase 100, grant new purchase 50, then spend 40 -> old 60/new 50, balance 110. History H2: grant old purchase 100, reserve/spend 100 from old, grant new purchase 50, spend 40 from new, **then refund the first reservation 100** -> old restored 100/new 10, balance 110 (or a new late restoration segment linked to the old paid order). The proposed `sum(grants)-Q=40` algorithm outputs old 60/new 50 for **both**. In H2 it retroactively reallocates 40 units of the later spend from new to old. The agreed late-gift policy disallows retroactive reallocation for a *late grant*; whether reversal of a prior ordinary spend may nevertheless rebase subsequent spending has **not** been decided. Treating the reservation refund as a fresh generic *nonrefundable* grant also discards the verified original purchase's refund eligibility. This is a material unagreed accounting semantic, not a stale-balance-only race: **waiting until the session ends does not restore the lost ordering**. If product policy explicitly allows netting/canonical retroactive attribution for reversed ordinary spends, the simple algorithm could be defined to return old 60/new 50, but this would require a new decision and changed examples/tests. Otherwise either record minimal reservation/spend source or ordered balance-transition evidence sufficient to attribute that reversal, or fail closed for histories with such overlaps (possibly permanently manual review). An active-request marker alone is not enough. This issue also applies to `WalletFunding.Settle(-delta)` and task/legacy wallet refunds, not only failure paths.

Source: `service/funding_source.go` `WalletFunding.PreConsume/Settle/Refund`, `service/billing_session.go` `Settle/Reserve/Refund` and `service/billing.go` `SettleBilling`. `BillingSession.Refund` sets an in-process flag *before* launching `gopool.Go`; the wallet refund uses a non-idempotent `IncreaseUserQuota` with no durable request record or retry. A process crash or failed async credit cannot be inferred from final `User.quota`; even a durable in-flight marker would need a terminal `failed/reconcile` state and operator disposition, never a timeout that asserts success. Trusted wallet sessions can initially reserve zero and later charge; register them before reporting the wallet settled. The legacy `SettleBilling` fallback and `PostConsumeQuota` (`service/quota.go`) and task billing (`service/task_billing.go:taskAdjustFunding`, `service/midjourney.go:RefundMidjourneyQuota`) also write wallet credits/debits outside the proposed `BillingSession` lifecycle. A single `BillingSession` hook is not universal. The `model/subscription.go:PurchaseSubscriptionWithBalance` debit is ordinary FIFO spending but requires the same lock/snapshot boundary; subscription-funded requests themselves do not debit the wallet.

`model/log.go:RecordConsumeLog` skips when `LogConsumeEnabled=false`, `createLog` writes `LOG_DB` separately and failure does not stop wallet mutation. These logs cannot fill the missing evidence. `model/quota_reserve.go` can reserve Redis balance first; `model/utils.go` queues per-user *aggregated, in-process* batch deltas and later flushes them, while `model/user_quota_cache_fence.go` keeps an **expiring** Redis pending marker. An active marker blocks snapshots while present, but after a crash between cache update and durable DB flush, the queue is gone and the marker/hash can expire; a later DB read can be stale with no durable proof that a delta was lost. Even without crash, a cleared batch marker says nothing about a still-running request that will refund or settle later. No-Redis mode writes wallet reserve/settle directly but still has the in-flight problem. Therefore exact quotes need a cross-instance, DB-persistent fail-closed wallet lifecycle/dirty signal and either a durable batch intent/flush acknowledgement or a verified synchronous DB path (or disable automatic quoting for all affected batch accounts). Redis TTL, cache fence and `GetUserQuota(..., true)` alone do not certify historical integrity. Quote and confirm must hold the same wallet fence plus user lock and revalidate all evidence *inside* the transaction; outside read is display-only. In particular, `model/user.go:DecreaseUserQuota` and `service/billing_session.go:reserveFunding` may drive quota negative on positive post-settle adjustments; a later top-up can hide that debt from a nonnegative final balance. Persist an untrusted/debt history signal or fail closed, not just `Q>=0`.

Other gate cases: a refund followed by a new purchase and consumption must use explicit committed removal/restoration events and wallet-locked re-quote, not `HandleCashbackIncident`; partial recovery of an already-spent old purchase can debit a newer purchase while recording a recovery against the old order, so its `CashbackQuotaMutation` cannot simply be subtracted from the old segment. Both go to manual review until their source/target semantics are proven. Same-second credits must be serialized by user-wallet row and assigned an unambiguous event order; second-resolution `CompleteTime`, `IssuedAt`, and `CreatedAt` do not suffice. An in-flight batch mutation means `retry_later`; lost/unknown batch history, negative wallet, an admin override, or source-specific debt means `manual_reconciliation` with no cash amount. A manual refund record needs a unique request/idempotency key, atomic wallet reservation and per-order paid-cents cap, plus explicit `pending_payout/paid/failed_manual_resolution` states: external cash transfer is not in the DB transaction and must never automatically repeat a wallet debit.

### Epay money and CNY provenance

`github.com/Calcium-Ion/go-epay/epay` v0.0.4 `epay/order.go:VerifyRes` exposes signed `Money` as a **string**, along with trade number/type/status, but no currency. `Verify` checks the signature; `epay/util.go:GenerateParams` includes nonempty `money` in the signed set. Both `controller/topup.go:EpayNotify` and `controller/topup_return.go:EpayBrowserReturn` currently pass only verified trade number/type to `model.RechargeEpay*`: signed `Money` is discarded before `model/topup.go:rechargeEpayWithSource` writes `TopUp` success/`CashbackOrderContext`, so *even a mismatch currently credits the order*. Pass the verified amount through **both** routes, parse positive decimal with at most two fractional digits to integer cents without float rounding, compare with the checkout amount encoded to two decimal places (`RequestEpay` sends `strconv.FormatFloat(payMoney,'f',2,64)` while `TopUp.Money` stores the unrounded `float64`; ideally persist expected integer cents at order creation), then store signed cents, gateway/merchant identity, order ID and an immutable CNY-attestation reference atomically with payment credit. Mismatch must roll back wallet and cashback, not merely suppress the quote. Other payment channels remain cashback-eligible and generate FIFO wallet-credit events but must not be auto-quoted as CNY.

`setting/operation_setting/payment_setting_old.go` and `model/option.go` show mutable `PayAddress`, `EpayId`, `EpayKey` options updated independently through `controller/option.go:UpdateOption`; `Verify` uses the *current* key. A Root assertion of CNY receipts is an auditable **business attestation**, not currency evidence in the callback. Bind an immutable confirmation to the merchant/gateway configuration generation and the order's verified payment transaction, invalidate on any gateway/merchant/key change in the same authoritative configuration boundary, and never apply a new assertion to a historical order. A separate volatile Boolean or non-transactional comparison of in-memory globals cannot prove this across concurrent updates/multiple nodes. Without documented actual CNY settlement confirmation, or for old orders lacking signed amount snapshot, show `manual_reconciliation` rather than a numeric CNY quote. `TopUp.Money` is expected checkout price (price/group/discount), not wallet quota or independently verified actual paid; other gateways may discount after checkout.

**Stage-gate recommendation:** Return the refund component to planning for a choice between (1) explicitly authorizing *net-rebased* accounting for reversible spends (and accepting retroactive reattribution), (2) adding enough per-reservation/adjustment evidence to preserve chronological provenance, with an audit of legacy/task paths and a durable batch integrity mechanism, or (3) permanently failing closed for accounts whose spending overlaps later credits/refunds or whose batch evidence cannot be proved, if that product limitation is acceptable. All three must still exclude old mixed balance, source-specific incidents/debt/override and unsupported currency from numeric automatic quotes. The current planned “only low-frequency grants + current balance + one active-session marker” is **not** proven sufficient; do not proceed to refund implementation or claim the acceptance criteria until the product rule and all database modes have been tested. This stage did not edit business code, provision databases or run integration/concurrency tests.
